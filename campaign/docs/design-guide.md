# Dweller Campaign — Design Guide

**Living document · v1.0 · Last updated November 21, 2026**
Reference build: `campaign/index.html` in `tlsman71/dweller`. Every design change updates this guide in the same pull request and adds a row to the [change log](#12-change-log). See [section 11](#11-how-this-guide-stays-current).

Dweller Campaign helps people thinking about running for local, state or federal office set up and run a campaign. It follows the **Dweller Docket design system** (Docket Design Guide v1.46): it should look like a trading terminal and read like an official filing guide. The Docket guide is the primary reference, not a cage: ideas that serve first-time candidates (the setup wizard, the milestone bar, the last-election snapshot) are welcome when they're built from the same tokens and patterns (Tom, Nov 21, 2026).

---

## 1. Principles

1. **Official source first.** Every rule, limit, deadline and figure links to the office that sets or publishes it: the FEC, the state election office or ethics commission, or the county or city clerk. If no official source has an item, the page says so and links the office. Nothing is filled from memory.
2. **Nonpartisan by design.** The same tools, wording and prominence for every party and for independents. No candidate or party is favored, and we publish no persuasion content. No party red or party blue in the interface; every party gets the same bar color in charts, listed in the order the official report prints them.
3. **Plain words, exact figures.** Labels a first-time candidate understands, with exact figures, units and dates.
4. **Live where the record is live.** Steps whose dates depend on where the candidate lives (ballot certification, election day, local filing) are filled in from the candidate's location, never guessed. Until a checked source exists they show "Date from your location" and link the office.
5. **Free public sources only.** The location lookup uses free official services (US Census Geocoder now; OpenFEC and Google Civic `elections`/`voterinfo` through a scheduled build). No paid AI search (Tom, Nov 21, 2026).
6. **Private by default.** What a candidate enters stays in their browser (`localStorage` key `dc-profile`). The street address goes only to the Census Bureau when they press Look up.
7. **Examples are labeled.** Until a candidate enters their own, the map and fundraising board show an **Example campaign** with a dashed EXAMPLE pill, and fictional names that start with "Example".

Text is never pure white, except the masthead tagline (`--tagline`). On dark surfaces text uses the amber-cream ramp `#F2D9B8` (strong) → `#E8D3B6` (body) → `#C8B497` (soft, hard-coded on the fundraising board). Docket-only colors are not carried over: no purple ALLEGED styling, no corruption-kind colors, no household-income greys.

| Role | Font | Style |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Body | IBM Plex Sans | 400, 15px, line-height 1.5 |
| Data, dates, deadlines, labels, figures | IBM Plex Mono | 400–600, tabular figures |

All numbers use tabular figures. No serif fonts.

## 4. Shape, spacing and motion

- 4px corners (`--r`) on cards, panels, buttons and inputs; pills fully rounded.
- 1px `--line` borders; chart gridlines dashed (2 on, 3 off).
- Up to 1280px wide, 16px side padding, 26px between sections.
- No animation beyond short transitions. With reduced motion on, the map lays itself out without moving and smooth scrolling is off.

## 5. Page anatomy (top to bottom)

1. **Masthead.** "Dweller Campaign" with Campaign in dollar green; mono ALL-CAPS tagline "Run for office, on the record" in `--tagline`; a 2px green rule. Controls: **Local / State / Federal** switch, **state** selector, **Light/Dark**. Picking a level or state changes the steps, dates, limits, sources and last-election data.
2. **Site search** under the masthead: steps, dates, map names, limits, setup steps, sections and every official source. ↑↓, Enter, Esc. A **Back to search** pill is pinned bottom-right once the search box scrolls out of view.
3. **Notice lede**: full-width surface panel with a 3px green left rule. Says what the page covers for the chosen level and state, where its rules come from and when they were checked, or that the state hasn't been checked yet with a link to its election office.
4. **Your campaign setup**: the five-step **milestone bar** (Mission & Office, District & Dates, Legal Readiness, Opponents & Win Number, Budget & Team) above one setup panel. Each step shows Done / Open / To do; the open step has an accent border. See 6.1.
5. **Key dates**: countdown cards ("in 13 days", "20 days ago **(passed)**"). The next upcoming date gets an accent border. Location-dependent dates are dashed cards: "Date from your location" with the office link.
6. **Campaign to-do list** (the Accountability board pattern): index tiles (steps done, now, next due date, past due), then one row per step in a scrolling frame: name and status, a step ladder, an owner pill (Candidate, Treasurer, Campaign manager, Volunteer), "Open since", due date with **(passed)** in bold, and the official source.
7. **Campaign map** (the Docket case map): one-line lede; search box, **↺ Show full map** (always visible), **Hide filters ▴** (filters open by default: Show chips by type with counts and shapes, and a Links switch: All links / Money / Filings and deadlines / People and groups); the green how-to strip; then the map and the 340px record panel side by side. The panel drops below the map at ≤ 860px. See 6.2.
8. **Last election for this seat**: see 6.3.
9. **Fundraising** (money board, always dark): see 6.4.
10. **Partners & sponsors**: no ads ever, and a standing note that sponsors have no say over content.
11. **Footer**: Follow Dweller pills (Bluesky @dwellerapp.bsky.social, Facebook, Instagram @joindweller) with generic line icons, then the sourcing note.

## 6. Components

### 6.1 Setup wizard

Step 1 opens with **"Let's start with why."** (kept from the v1 prototype). Each step has a mono kicker ("STEP 2 OF 5 · DISTRICT & DATES"), a Plex Sans heading, a plain hint, fields with mono ALL-CAPS labels, and **← Back** / **Save and continue →** pills.

- **District & Dates** asks for street address and ZIP (the Census Geocoder needs a street; a ZIP alone isn't enough) and calls the geocoder by JSONP, since it doesn't allow CORS. Results show in a soft green result box with the source and lookup date. The state selector follows the result.
- **Legal Readiness** is the ladder for the level as a checklist: tick, date, the step in plain words and its source. Live steps carry a dashed LIVE pill.
- **Opponents & Win Number** lists others running the same way for everyone, and computes the win number as arithmetic (more than half of the expected votes in a two-way race), offering last election's vote count when we have it. It's labeled a planning figure, not a rule.
- **Budget & Team** sets the goal and treasurer and adds team members, endorsers and vendors to the map.

### 6.2 Record panel

Before a pick: HOW TO READ THIS, **Tap any dot**, and three bullets (what a tap shows; the shape and color legend in words; green money lines, dotted filing lines, thin people lines). After a pick: a full-width **← Back to the full map** button, the type with its shape, the name, facts, and **Connected to** links. A round **✕ Show the full map** pill floats at the map's top-left. Either button, Esc, or a tap on empty map clears the pick.

### 6.3 Last election snapshot

For the seat and district the candidate picked: tiles for votes for this office, registered voters (with the report's date), registered voters with no vote for this office (explained as registered minus votes, which also counts people who skipped the race), and mail ballots for the seat. Then the results table (party as printed) and a registered-by-party bar list with **Show as table**. Figures that the office doesn't publish for the district say "Not published", explain why, and give the statewide figure with its source. Until a seat is matched, California's 1st Assembly District (2024) is shown with an EXAMPLE pill.

### 6.4 Fundraising board

Always dark. One big number (cash on hand), progress toward the goal, tiles for raised, spent, cash on hand and debts owed, and the **contribution limits** for the level and state beside them, with cycle, notes and source. A monthly raised/spent bar chart with hover values and **Show as table**. A ledger form adds entries (kept in the browser). A **⚑ Check** line appears when one donor's total passes the per-election limit, with a note that the limit is per election.

### 6.5 Pills and tags

Owner pill (mono caps, rounded), EXAMPLE pill (dashed), LIVE pill (dashed), Done/Now/To do meta in mono caps.

## 7. Wording rules

- Plain labels: "Filed to run", "On the ballot", "Cash on hand", not jargon.
- Exact figures with units and dates: "$3,500 per election", "within 10 days of raising or spending $2,000".
- Every rule, limit and deadline links to the office that sets it. If none, say so and name the office.
- No odds, predictions, endorsements or persuasion.

## 8. Ladders

| Level | Steps | Model and sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal | Exploring → Became a candidate → Filed to run → Committee registered → On the ballot (live) → Reports during the race → Election day (live) → Post-election report → Closed out | FEC |
| State | Exploring → Intention filed → Committee formed → Filed to run → On the ballot (live) → Primary → Election day (live) → Post-election reports and close-out (live) | California State Assembly: FPPC Manual 1, CA SOS 2026 Assembly sheet |
| Local | Exploring → Intention filed → Committee formed → Filed to run (live) → On the ballot (live) → Election day (live) → Post-election reports → Closed out | California city and county: FPPC Manual 2 and local checklist |

Other states use the same step names; until their sources are checked the page says so and links the state's election office.

## 9. Accessibility

- Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1; meaningful marks ≥ 3:1. Color is never the only cue (shapes, words, bold "(passed)").
- No sideways scrolling at 390px; tables scroll inside their own frame.
- Scrolling frames (`--wash`, `--line` border) are keyboard-focusable. Map dots are focusable buttons (Enter or Space picks).
- Visible focus rings in `--focus`. Reduced motion respected.

## 10. Data

`campaign/data.js` holds sources, ladders, dates, limits and last-election results. Each entry cites a source key; the page never shows an item without one. Results figures are copied from the official report, never computed, except "registered, no vote for this office", which is labeled as a subtraction.

## 11. How this guide stays current

Whenever a change affects the look or behavior of the page:

1. Update this guide in the same pull request.
2. Regenerate the token table: `node campaign/scripts/sync-design-guide.mjs`. Never edit the table by hand; change the CSS custom properties in `campaign/index.html`.
3. Add a change-log row at the top of section 12: minor version for a visible design change, major for a new direction. Use `pending` for the commit until it exists.
4. Re-check changed chart or node colors with the dataviz validator against `#1E2124` and the light theme, and note the result in the row.

CI (`.github/workflows/campaign-design-guide.yml`) fails a pull request when the token table doesn't match the code, or when `campaign/index.html` changed without a new change-log row. Label a PR `no-design-change` for logic-only or data-only fixes.
